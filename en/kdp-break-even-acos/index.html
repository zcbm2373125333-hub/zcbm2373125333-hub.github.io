What is my break-even ACOS on Amazon KDP ads?

Last updated: 2026-10-19 · By the StraightGate Finance team

Short answer: Your break-even ACOS is your royalty per sale divided by the book's list price: below it, Amazon ads make you money; above it, each ad-driven sale loses money (before Kindle Unlimited reads and read-through). Because royalties differ so much, a $4.99 eBook on the 70% option breaks even at about 68% ACOS, while a $12.99 paperback with $3.50 printing breaks even at about 33%.

Worked example

Three books sold on Amazon.com (no VAT). eBook file size 1 MB (delivery $0.15/MB). Paperback printing cost $3.50 is an assumption – use the figure from your KDP pricing page. Conversion rate for the max-CPC column: 10% of clicks become orders.

At a 45% ACOS, the $4.99 eBook still earns about $1.14 per ad-driven sale ($3.39 − 45% × $4.99), while the paperback loses about $1.55 ($4.29 − $5.85).

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ACOS for KDP ads?

Any ACOS below your break-even ACOS is profitable on that sale. Compare each campaign with the break-even figure for that specific book and format, not with a generic target.

Can ACOS above 100% still be profitable?

Yes, for series and Kindle Unlimited books. Amazon's ad sales don't include KU page reads or later books in the series, so read-through can make a high ACOS worthwhile. Add expected read-through profit to the royalty to see it.

How do I calculate break-even CPC?

Break-even CPC = royalty per sale × conversion rate (orders ÷ clicks). With a $3.39 royalty and 10% conversion, you can pay up to $0.34 per click.

Why is ACOS based on list price, not royalty?

Amazon Ads reports sales at the customer price. Your profit, however, is the royalty – that's why break-even ACOS equals royalty ÷ price.

Will Vinted, eBay or Wallapop report me to the tax office? The DAC7 30-sales / €2,000 rule explained

Last updated: 2026-10-19 · By the StraightGate Finance team · Other languages: Deutsch Español Français Italiano

Short answer: Under the EU's DAC7 rules (Directive 2021/514), a marketplace such as Vinted, eBay, Wallapop, Leboncoin, Subito or Kleinanzeigen must report you to the tax authority if, on that one platform in one calendar year, you make 30 or more sales of goods or receive more than €2,000. If you stay below both limits on a platform, that platform does not report you – and being reported is not a tax bill in itself.

Worked example

Illustrative numbers, not a real person's data.

Anna lives in Germany and clears out her flat in 2026:

  • Vinted: 28 sales, €640 received
  • eBay: 9 sales, €1,450 received
  • Kleinanzeigen: 4 sales paid with “Sicher bezahlen” (€180) plus 6 cash sales on collection

In total that is 47 sales and well over €2,000 – but no platform reports her, because each platform counts on its own and none of them reaches 30 sales or €2,000. Cash sales outside the platform's payment system are generally not counted.

If she sells two more items on Vinted in December, Vinted reaches 30 sales and reports her by 31 January 2027. If instead she sells one camera on eBay for €600, her eBay total becomes €2,050 and eBay reports her.

What exactly gets reported?

For each reportable seller the platform sends the tax authority your name, address, tax identification number, date of birth, bank or payment account, the number of sales and the amounts paid to you per quarter, and the fees it withheld. The platform must give you the same information. Tax authorities then exchange the data between EU countries (European Commission).

Is the €2,000 before or after fees?

The amount is the “consideration” paid or credited to you, net of fees the platform withholds. Italy's Agenzia delle Entrate states this explicitly in its DAC7 FAQ (source). In practice: count what lands in your wallet, not the price shown on the listing.

Germany: €2,000 exactly already counts

Germany's PStTG excludes sellers with fewer than 30 sales and less than €2,000, so a German seller who receives exactly €2,000 on one platform is reported (PStTG, BZSt FAQ).

Frequently asked questions

Do my Vinted and eBay sales add up?

No. The 30-sale and €2,000 limits apply to each platform separately. You can sell on five platforms and never be reported, as long as each stays under both limits.

Does being reported mean I have to pay tax?

No. DAC7 only makes platforms share data. Whether you owe tax depends on your country's rules – selling your own used belongings for less than you paid is generally not taxable in most EU countries, while buying to resell at a profit can be business income. France's tax office says DAC7 changes nothing about existing taxation.

When will the platform report me?

By 31 January of the following year, for the previous calendar year. The platform will ask you for your tax ID and address first if you are close to or over a limit.

Do cash sales on collection count?

Generally no. Platforms count sales they process. Kleinanzeigen counts only “Sicher bezahlen”, Subito only TuttoSubito and Wallapop only wallet-paid sales.

Is the threshold going to change?

Possibly from 2028. On 24 June 2026 the European Commission proposed removing the 30-sale test and raising the amount to €3,000, but it is only a proposal and needs unanimous approval by EU governments. For 2026 and 2027 the 30 / €2,000 rule applies.

Does DAC7 apply outside the EU (UK, Switzerland)?

DAC7 is EU law. The UK has its own, similar OECD-based platform reporting rules; this page covers EU platforms and EU-resident sellers.

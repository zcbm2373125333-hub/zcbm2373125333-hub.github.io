Is the DAC7 reporting threshold changing to €3,000 with no 30-sales limit?

Last updated: 2026-10-19 · By the StraightGate Finance team

Short answer: Not yet. On 24 June 2026 the European Commission proposed (COM(2026) 308) to remove the 30-sale test for sellers of goods and raise the amount to €3,000 per platform per year, to apply from 1 January 2028 at the earliest. It needs unanimous approval by EU governments, so for 2026 and 2027 the current rule – 30 sales or more than €2,000 – still applies.

The Commission's factsheet says the higher threshold would remove reporting obligations for over 10 million private sellers, particularly people selling second-hand goods (factsheet). The change sits inside a larger “recast” that merges DAC1–DAC9 into one directive (EUR-Lex).

Frequently asked questions

Does the €3,000 rule apply to my 2026 sales?

No. 2026 and 2027 sales are reported under the current 30 sales / €2,000 rule.

Will the proposal definitely pass?

Unknown. Tax directives need unanimous agreement of EU member states, and the text can change during negotiation.

Does it change rentals or services?

The proposed change targets the sale of goods. Other DAC7 activities (property rentals, personal services, transport) keep their own rules.

Should I change my spreadsheet now?

How much do freelancers actually keep from a $1,000 Upwork or Fiverr job?

Last updated: 2026-10-19 · By the StraightGate Finance team · Other languages: Deutsch Español

Short answer: On Fiverr you keep 80% of each completed order, so a $1,000 order pays you $800 before withdrawal or currency costs. On Upwork the Freelancer Service Fee is set per contract between 0% and 15%, so the same $1,000 leaves you between $850 and $1,000 – and converting the money into your own currency typically costs another few percent.

Why the “real” fee is higher than the headline fee

Freelancers usually lose money in three places: the marketplace fee, the payment or withdrawal method, and the exchange rate when the money is converted into their home currency. The marketplace fee is the only one most people track. A simple per-payment log of gross amount, each fee and the amount that actually arrived shows your true take-home rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is Upwork's fee still a flat 10%?

No. Upwork's help centre now says the Freelancer Service Fee ranges from 0% to 15% per contract. You see the percentage before you submit a proposal or accept an offer, and it doesn't change during the contract.

Does Fiverr take 20% of tips?

Yes. Fiverr says you earn 80% of the purchase amount, including Gig Extras and tips.

Who pays Fiverr's 5.5% service fee?

The buyer. It is added at checkout (plus $3.50 on orders under $200) and doesn't reduce the freelancer's 80%.

How can I reduce currency conversion costs?

Compare the conversion rate you actually receive with the mid-market rate for a few payments. Holding a balance in the client's currency or using a cheaper conversion route can matter more than the platform fee on large invoices.

Does Upwork refund its fee if I refund the client?

Yes. Upwork returns the Freelancer Service Fee on any amount refunded to the client.

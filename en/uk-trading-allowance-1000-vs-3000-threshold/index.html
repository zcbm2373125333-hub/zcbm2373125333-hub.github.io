Is the UK side-hustle tax limit £1,000 or £3,000?

Last updated: 2026-10-20 · By the StraightGate Finance team

Short answer: It is still £1,000: if your gross side-hustle (trading) income in a tax year is over £1,000 you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends, and the £1,000 trading allowance is the only tax-free amount. The £3,000 figure is a reporting threshold the government announced in March 2025 for 'within this Parliament' with no start date set as of October 2026 – and even then income between £1,000 and £3,000 stays taxable, it would just be reported through a new online service instead of a tax return.

Worked example

Illustrative numbers, not a real person's data.

Illustrative only. Priya, employed, sells handmade candles on Etsy and at markets in tax year 2025/26 (6 April 2025 – 5 April 2026): gross takings £2,400, materials and fees £300.

StepOption A: trading allowanceOption B: actual expenses
Gross income£2,400£2,400
Deduct£1,000 allowance£300 expenses
Taxable profit£1,400£2,100

She picks the allowance (lower profit). Because gross income is over £1,000 she must register for Self Assessment by 5 October 2026 and file by 31 January 2027. Assuming her salary already uses her personal allowance and she is a basic-rate (20%) taxpayer, that is £280 income tax on £1,400; the profit is below the Class 4 NIC threshold. If the £3,000 reporting threshold were already in force, she would skip the tax return – but still owe the same £280.

Separately: if Priya made 30+ sales or more than €2,000 (about £1,700) on Etsy, Etsy reports her to HMRC under the digital platform rules. That report is information, not a bill.

What counts as trading income?

Self-employment, casual services such as babysitting or gardening, and hiring out personal equipment (GOV.UK). Selling your own unwanted possessions, usually at a loss, is generally not trading – but buying to resell is.

When you must register even under £1,000

To claim a loss, pay voluntary Class 2 NIC, or claim Tax-Free Childcare or Maternity Allowance based on self-employment (GOV.UK).

Frequently asked questions

Is the first £3,000 of side-hustle income tax-free?

No. Only £1,000 is tax-free via the trading allowance. The £3,000 is a planned reporting threshold; tax would still be due above £1,000.

When does the £3,000 threshold start?

No date has been set. The government said 'within this Parliament' (11 March 2025 and 28 April 2025). Until it is legislated, the £1,000 rule applies.

Is the £1,000 per platform?

No. It is per person, across all your trades combined, per tax year.

Will eBay or Vinted report me to HMRC?

Platforms report sellers who make 30 or more sales of goods or receive more than about £1,700 (€2,000) in a year. A report does not mean you owe tax.

Can I use the allowance and deduct expenses?

No – it is one or the other. Use the £1,000 allowance when your real expenses are lower than £1,000.

Why is my Upwork fee 15% – and who pays less?

Last updated: 2026-10-20 · By the StraightGate Finance team

Short answer: Since 1 May 2025, Upwork sets the Freelancer Service Fee per new contract anywhere from 0% to 15%, based on factors such as client demand for that type of work, so many offers now show 15%. The rate is shown before you send a proposal or accept an offer and is locked for the life of the contract; to take home a target amount, bill target ÷ (1 − fee).

Worked example

Illustrative numbers, not a real person's data.

A developer wants to take home $20/hour, or $1,700 on a fixed-price job. Withdrawal and currency costs ignored.

Contract feeHourly rate to bill for $20 netNet from a $2,000 fixed job
0%$20.00$2,000.00
10%$22.22 (Upwork's own example)$1,800.00

At 15%, a contract billed at the old 10%-era rate of $22.22 nets $18.89/hour – 5.6% less than planned.

Frequently asked questions

Can I negotiate the Upwork fee down?

Not directly. Upwork says it offers no regular discounts, only occasional promotions it will notify you about. You can only change what you bill.

Does the fee change during a long contract?

No. Once the contract starts the percentage stays fixed for the life of that contract.

Where do I see the fee?

In the offer or proposal screen before you commit, and later in contract details and transaction summaries.

Does Upwork refund its fee on refunds?

Yes, Upwork returns the Freelancer Service Fee on amounts refunded to the client.

Is there also a withdrawal fee?

It depends on the method. Since 1 September 2026, Direct to U.S. Bank withdrawals cost $2.99 each if your tax address is outside the United States (still free with a US tax address). Add that to your gross-up when you withdraw small amounts often.
